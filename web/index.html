    About rerouter

    rerouter is a really easy way to keep a library of your routes (gravel, road, mountain biking, hiking) and to do, in one click, the things riders keep needing and keep doing by hand: combine two routes into a new ride, start a loop somewhere else, check the weather along the way, find the cafés and water taps on a route, or plan a day out with the train.

    It runs in your browser. Your routes stay with you: in this browser on this device, or on your own rerouter server at home (see Library & settings). Nothing is uploaded to rerouter; the services it asks for directions, places, weather and trains only get the points they need.

    How to use it

    1. Import your routes (Import): drop GPX, TCX or FIT files, whole folders or zip files, and set their source, activity and tags. Or paste a link to a route on RideWithGPS, Komoot or Strava (Utilities › Import from a link). Just looking around? Add a set of example routes on the Import screen (while your library is empty) or under Library & settings. rerouter works out the distance, the climbing, a name after the places the route visits and, if you like, how much of it is paved.
    2. Manage your library (Library): filter the table (distance, climbing, paved, quality, tags) and use Browse on the left for activity, source, collections, areas and regions. Click a route for its panel: map, elevation profile, surface, the places along it, rating, tags and notes, and Share…. Tick several routes to tag them, put them in a collection, download or export them. Utilities › Duplicates cleans up routes you have twice.
    3. See them on the map (Map): every route that matches your filters, in the map style you like (the layer button, top right; Grey map makes the routes stand out). Highlight routes near each other shows where routes meet: good starting points for a combination.
    4. Add places (Places): import your Google My Maps or a CSV file, add example lists, or click ＋ Place on the map. Places show on every map and in each route's panel (Places along this route), where you can also look up more on OpenStreetMap.
    5. Use the utilities (Utilities): Combine routes into a new ride with connectors routed for you, Change start point of a loop, Ride weather for the day you ride, Search for places along a route (and ride past them), and Train rides: a day out with the train, with your routes as the riding part. What you make can be saved as a new route or downloaded as GPX for your bike computer.

    Keep a backup now and then (Library & settings › Download backup), certainly when your library lives in this browser.

              Your route library, in your browser

              rerouter keeps a library of GPX routes for gravel, road and mountain biking, and hiking: stats, a map, filters, finding routes that overlap, and combining two routes into a new one.

              Everything you add is stored in this browser only, on this device. Nothing is uploaded. The connector routing and the surface estimate ask a BRouter server for directions (the public one at brouter.de by default); only the points being routed are sent. The ride weather asks Open-Meteo for the forecast at a few points along the route.

              Your library stays when you refresh the page (even a hard refresh) or close the browser, but not when you clear this site's data or use a private window. So download a backup now and then (⚙ Library & settings, top right).

              You're using Safari. Safari deletes the data of a website after 7 days of using Safari without visiting that site, so in Safari your library only lasts while you use rerouter at least once a week. Keep a backup, or use another browser (Firefox, Chrome, Edge) for rerouter.

              Where your routes are

              Your library lives in this browser's storage on this device: .

              Safe: refreshing the page (a hard refresh too), closing the tab or the browser, restarting the computer, and new versions of rerouter.

              Gone, or out of reach:

              • when you clear this site's data ("cookies and other site data" when clearing browsing data);
              • in a private or incognito window: everything goes when the window closes;
              • in Safari (Mac, iPhone, iPad): after 7 days of using Safari without opening rerouter;
              • when the disk is nearly full and the browser frees up space (unless it agreed to keep it, see above);
              • in another browser, browser profile or device: each has its own, separate library.

              The library belongs to the address : clearing the site data of that address clears it, also for other pages on the same address.

              So: download a backup now and then, and keep it outside the browser (e.g. in a cloud folder). A backup also moves the library to another browser or device.

              Backup

              One zip file with every route, your ratings, tags and notes, and all original GPX files. The browser version and the server version use the same backups, so this also moves a library between them.

              To share only some routes, select them in the library and choose Export set…. Such a zip (or a whole backup) can also be added to a library, keeping the routes that are already there.

              Example routes

              Sets of routes to start with. They are added to your library (routes you already have are skipped).

              Settings

              The public server at brouter.de is shared by many people: please don't estimate the surface of hundreds of routes in one go there. Running your own BRouter (e.g. with docker) and filling in its address here works too, as long as it allows requests from this page (CORS).

              Home

              Where you live, so that shared routes leave out their start and end near it (a route from your front door otherwise shows where that is). It stays in your library and is never shared. Click the map to set it.

              Start over

              Remove every route and file from this browser. This cannot be undone: download a backup first.
